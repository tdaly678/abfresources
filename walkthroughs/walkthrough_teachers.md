## Section 1 — Signing in (and "Add yourself as a teacher")

The site is at **abfresources.com**. It's a private hub — there's a password gate on the front page so only leaders and teachers get in. Sign in with the password Tom shares with you.

Once you're inside, click your name in the top right to identify as a teacher. The site will show you the teacher views — your four tabs.

**New here?** If you don't see your name on the roster yet, look for the **"Add yourself as a teacher"** option on the homepage. You'll fill out a quick profile and you're on. ABF leaders will be able to find you, see what courses you've got ready, and send you teaching requests.

## Section 3 — The four teacher tabs (the main event)

When you sign in as a teacher you have **four tabs** that together replace every back-and-forth text exchange you used to have with ABF leaders about scheduling. Walk through them in this order:

### 1) My Availability — the Sunday grid

A grid of every Sunday from September through June (the regular ABF year — summer is shaded out). Click each Sunday to cycle through:
- **Available** (green)
- **Tentative** (gold)
- **Unavailable** (red)
- *No answer* (blank, click again to clear)

Sundays the church doesn't meet — Family Worship Sundays, Easter, the summer break — are diagonally striped. You can ignore those.

**Why this matters:** ABF leaders see this grid when they're choosing teachers. Filling out availability for the year, even at "tentative", lets leaders plan ahead instead of scrambling two weeks before a series starts. Set it once. Adjust as life changes.

### 2) Incoming Requests — leaders asking for you

When an ABF leader sends you a teaching request, it shows up here. You'll see:
- Who asked
- Which ABF
- Which Sunday(s) they want
- The course or topic
- A short personal note

You **accept** or **decline** with one click. If you accept, those Sundays get flagged as booked. If you decline, no hard feelings — leaders just keep looking.

### 3) My Courses — what you have ready to teach

This is where you list the teaching series you can offer. Each course has:
- A title (e.g. "Joshua")
- A short description
- **Min weeks** and **max weeks** — the realistic range a series like this runs

**Why min/max matters:** ABF leaders see your courses when they're picking who to ask. The week range tells them how a series will fit in their fall, winter, or spring slot. A 9–11 week Joshua series fits a 10-Sunday window. A 4–6 week series fits a different one. You're saving leaders the work of guessing.

You can add as many courses as you want, and edit them anytime.

### 4) My Profile — the human part

This is the page leaders see when they click your name on the availability grid. Don't skip it. The fields:
- **Name, email, phone** (phone is optional)
- **Year you began attending LEFC**
- **Member of LEFC?** (yes/no)
- **Approximately how many weeks have you taught?** — your stage (Up & Comer / Active / Veteran) is set automatically from this answer
- **Venues you've taught in** (LEFC ABFs, other churches, conferences, etc.)
- **Relevant training** (formal and informal)
- **A short "get to know me" line** (140 characters)
- **A longer bio** (optional)
- **Doctrinal alignment** — a checkbox: *"I have read and affirm LEFC's Statement of Faith and Matters of Faith & Practice, and will teach in alignment with both."* Standard for any LEFC teaching role.

A complete profile helps ABF leaders match you with classes and topics that fit.

## Section 4 — Why all this matters

The old way of being a teacher at LEFC meant sitting on a list, hoping the right ABF leader thought of you, then doing the back-and-forth dance over text to land a date. Months would slip and good teachers wouldn't get scheduled. Or you'd say yes to something on a Tuesday morning text and then realize on Saturday it conflicted with a kid's event.

This tool flips the script. You're saying *upfront* when you're available, what you can teach, and how leaders can reach you. ABF leaders look at the grid, find a fit, and ask cleanly. You accept or decline cleanly. Everyone uses fewer words and fewer texts. And the teaching year actually gets planned in advance — instead of scrambled together a few weeks at a time.

## Section 5 — The other features (brief lap)

Even though the focus today is signing in and scheduling:

- **Resources for teachers** — there's a "For New Teachers" video set worth watching: The Up & Comers Process, Elder Pairing & Teaching Prep, and Discussion-Based Teaching. If you're newer at this, start there.
- **Online courses** — full Bible study series like Joshua (WBF Spring 2026) with PDF slides and Spotify audio, in case you want to see how a full series gets organized.
- **Class Feedback** — when you finish a teaching series, the Feedback tab lets you launch a survey for the class with a QR code they can scan from their phones. Anonymous, structured feedback that actually helps you grow as a teacher.

## Suggested narrative arc for the video

A recommended structure that fits 3–5 minutes:

1. **Open (~20s):** "If you teach in an ABF at LEFC — or you'd like to — here's how to sign in and put yourself on the schedule."
2. **Sign in + role pick (~20s):** Password gate. Click your name to identify as a teacher. New? Use "Add yourself as a teacher."

4. **Incoming Requests (~30s):** What it looks like when an ABF leader asks you to teach. Accept or decline.
5. **My Courses (~45s):** Add what you can teach. Min/max weeks help leaders plan.
6. **My Profile (~30s):** Fill it out so leaders can match you with classes. Note the doctrinal alignment statement.
7. **Why this matters (~20s):** Plan the teaching year in advance instead of scrambling.
8. **Quick lap (~20s):** Mention training videos, online courses, Feedback tool.
9. **Close (~15s):** "Sign in. Set availability for the year. Add one course. That's a strong first day."
